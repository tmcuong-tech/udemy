## Course Goal

This course helps learners prepare for CISSP by applying security leadership judgment across the eight domains. The labs are written to reinforce a manager-first mindset: protect the business, manage risk, choose proportionate controls, document decisions, and communicate clearly with stakeholders.

## Prerequisites

- Basic IT, networking, and systems administration awareness.
- At least one year of working experience is recommended for this course.
- Ability to operate a computer and use browser-based tools.
- Willingness to write short security justifications, not only technical answers.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, learners should be able to:

- Apply governance, ethics, policy, risk, and compliance concepts.
- Classify information assets and choose lifecycle controls.
- Explain secure architecture, design principles, cryptography, and physical security.
- Design secure networks and communication channels.
- Manage identity lifecycle, authentication, authorization, and access reviews.
- Plan security assessments, audits, metrics, and remediation tracking.
- Coordinate security operations, incident response, logging, monitoring, backup, and recovery.
- Integrate security into software development and DevSecOps.
- Solve cross-domain security scenarios using CISSP-style reasoning.

## Lab Working Method

Use this method in every lab:

1. Read the scenario.
2. Identify assets, stakeholders, threats, vulnerabilities, and business impact.
3. Choose controls by risk priority.
4. Record assumptions.
5. Write the security decision in plain business language.
6. Check whether the answer balances confidentiality, integrity, availability, safety, compliance, and cost.

## CISSP Answering Mindset

When multiple answers look correct:

1. Prefer governance before implementation.
2. Prefer risk assessment before tool selection.
3. Prefer policy and process before ad hoc technical action.
4. Prefer least privilege, defense in depth, and separation of duties.
5. Prefer business continuity and safety where availability is critical.
6. Prefer evidence preservation during incidents.
7. Prefer the answer that a security manager should choose, not only the answer an engineer can execute.

Each learner should maintain:

- Risk register.
- Asset classification table.
- Network and security architecture diagrams.
- IAM lifecycle matrix.
- Assessment and audit plan.
- Incident response checklist.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery notes.
- Secure SDLC checklist.
- Final cross-domain security recommendation.

## Assessment Activities

Learners should demonstrate:

- Correct use of CISSP domain language.
- Ability to reason through scenario-based questions.
- Practical understanding of control types.
- Ability to justify security decisions to management.
- Ability to connect technical controls with business risk.

## Final Exam Readiness Checklist

Before attempting the CISSP exam, confirm that you can:

- Explain the eight CISSP domains and their relationships.
- Distinguish administrative, technical, and physical controls.
- Distinguish preventive, detective, corrective, deterrent, recovery, and compensating controls.
- Apply risk treatment options: mitigate, transfer, avoid, accept.
- Explain data classification, ownership, retention, and destruction.
- Explain cryptography, PKI, secure architecture, and network security concepts.
- Explain IAM models, federation, SSO, MFA, and privileged access.
- Explain assessment, audit, vulnerability management, and penetration testing differences.
- Explain incident response, logging, SIEM, change management, BCP, and DR.
- Explain secure SDLC, software testing, supply chain risk, and DevSecOps.
