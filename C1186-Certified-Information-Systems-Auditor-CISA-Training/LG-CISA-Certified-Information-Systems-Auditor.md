# Learner Guide: C1186 Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) Training

This guide supports a 5-day CISA course for learners preparing for information systems audit, control, assurance, governance, risk, and security responsibilities. The labs are aligned to the five CISA domains described in the reference guide.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

- Plan an information systems audit using objectives, scope, materiality, and risk-based planning.
- Collect, evaluate, and document audit evidence using sampling and data analytics techniques.
- Write practical audit findings with risk, evidence, recommendation, owner, and follow-up status.
- Review IT governance, strategy alignment, policies, risk management, portfolio management, and performance monitoring.
- Audit system acquisition, SDLC, Agile, DevSecOps, change management, application controls, testing, migration, and post-implementation activities.
- Assess IT operations, database administration, incident management, problem management, cloud operations, and service management controls.
- Evaluate business continuity and disaster recovery plans using BIA, RTO, RPO, recovery strategies, and test evidence.
- Review protection of information assets through access control, IAM, encryption, network security, data classification, privacy, vulnerability management, and physical security.
- Apply the CISA mindset: think like an auditor, evaluate risk, test controls, and recommend improvements.

### Step 2: Use Standard Audit Fields

For every lab artifact, capture:

- Audit objective
- Scope
- Criteria
- Risk
- Control
- Evidence requested
- Evidence received
- Test performed
- Exception noted
- Impact
- Recommendation
- Owner
- Target date
- Follow-up status

### Step 3: Apply the Auditor Mindset

When answering lab scenarios:

1. Identify the risk first.
2. Identify the control objective.
3. Determine whether evidence is sufficient, reliable, relevant, and timely.
4. Test the control before forming a conclusion.
5. Recommend management action rather than implementing the fix yourself.

## Domain Study Path

### Domain 1: Information Systems Auditing Process

Complete Labs 01 and 02.

Focus on audit standards, ethics, audit planning, risk assessment, materiality, evidence, sampling, CAATs, reporting, and follow-up.

Checkpoint questions:

1. What makes audit evidence sufficient and appropriate?
2. How does risk-based planning affect audit scope?
3. When would sampling or CAATs be used?
4. What should every audit finding include?

### Domain 2: Governance and Management of IT

Complete Labs 03 and 04.

Focus on governance frameworks, IT strategy alignment, roles, policies, standards, risk management, resource management, portfolio management, quality assurance, and performance metrics.

Checkpoint questions:

1. How does IT governance support business objectives?
2. What evidence proves policies are approved, communicated, and reviewed?
3. How should IT risk be reported to management?
4. What makes a KPI useful for governance?

### Domain 3: IS Acquisition, Development, and Implementation

Complete Labs 05 and 06.

Focus on SDLC, Agile, DevSecOps, requirements, feasibility, project governance, application controls, testing, change management, data migration, conversion, and post-implementation review.

Checkpoint questions:

1. Which controls belong early in the SDLC?
2. What evidence supports effective change management?
3. How do input, processing, and output controls differ?
4. Why is post-implementation review important?

### Domain 4: IS Operations and Business Resilience

Complete Labs 07 and 08.

Focus on IT service management, operations, database administration, incident and problem management, SLA monitoring, cloud operations, virtualization, BIA, BCP, DRP, RTO, RPO, and recovery testing.

Checkpoint questions:

1. How do incident and problem management differ?
2. What evidence proves operational controls are working?
3. How do RTO and RPO drive recovery strategy?
4. What makes a BCP/DR test meaningful?

### Domain 5: Protection of Information Assets

Complete Labs 09 and 10.

Focus on information security governance, IAM, access control models, network security, encryption, PKI, data classification, privacy, vulnerability management, physical security, security awareness, and social engineering defenses.

Checkpoint questions:

1. How does least privilege reduce risk?
2. What evidence supports a strong IAM review?
3. How should vulnerabilities be prioritized and tracked?
4. How do physical, technical, and administrative controls work together?

## Final Readiness Checklist

Before the exam, confirm you can:

1. Explain all five CISA domains and their relative weights.
2. Distinguish risk, control, control objective, evidence, finding, and recommendation.
3. Select the best auditor action in scenario questions.
4. Avoid answering like the system owner or engineer.
5. Prioritize governance, risk, evidence, independence, and control effectiveness.
